## The unit bug, and the four defects it exposed

Andy: "Picking a +2400 as a POTD with 70 conviction is not correct."

He was right, and it was mine. `signal_registry.hit_rate` stores a
**percent** (measured: 978 of 978 non-NCAAB rows hold 6.6..100.0, median
52.4). `set_ncaab_signal_baseline.py` wrote **fractions**, and
`_resolve_weight` does `hr = float(reg['hit_rate']) / 100.0` — so 91.07%
arrived at the scorer as **0.9%**.

Every NCAAB signal then tripped the auto-fade in `ensemble_scorer.py`:

    elif tier == 'ANTI_VALIDATED' and hr is not None and n >= 25 and hr <= 0.47:

With `hr` two orders of magnitude too small, *every* ANTI_VALIDATED NCAAB
signal faded — at `fade_hr = 1.0 - hr` ≈ 99%, weights 0.563 and 0.444,
onto the **opposite side**. That is how a 24-to-1 longshot became the
engine's top-ranked play with conviction 70. Fixed; hit rates now read
38.98–92.59 and the fade fires only where it is meant to
(`ncaab_away_spread_edge` 46.74% n=92, `home_ats_cold_at_home` 47.18%
n=142).

Clearing the corrupted output then hit four more defects in a row, each
of which had been latent:

1. **`min_lead` applied to a self-refresh.** The POTD override compares
   its candidate against the incumbent's conviction. When the incumbent
   is its *own* earlier write both numbers come from the same scorer, so
   the comparison asked whether tonight's engine beats its own corrupted
   run by 5 points. It did not (56 vs an inflated 62), so the stale
   `Santa Clara RL` held the surface. A refresh replaces; it does not win
   an election.

2. **`merge-duplicates` without `on_conflict` is insert-only.** Both
   `jerry_cache` writes in the override posted to the bare table, so any
   write landing on an existing `best_bet_{today}` returned 409 23505 —
   and the function returns on a publish failure, so the history write
   never ran either. The override could only ever *create* a POTD, never
   replace one. Every other `jerry_cache` writer already uses
   `on_conflict=cache_key`.

3. **The Sharp's publish lock ranked a corrupted deck above a correct
   one.** The lock compares `(PRIME, STRONG, total conviction)`. The
   corrupted deck's inflated convictions summed to 292 against the
   corrected 213, so "not better than current" held three picks that
   existed in **no read at all** (Santa Clara -17.5 @ 62, Auburn -26.5 @
   62, Saint Joseph's -9.5 @ 60 — all three games now resolve to ML at
   conviction 50). The void sweep that would have caught this was scoped
   to props only, though sides fail identically: the composer reads
   type/line/side straight off `primary_play`. Added a **stale** purge
   keyed on the game's current read, and a **newly-priced** purge for the
   next case down (same pick, published unpriced, fresh copy priced —
   invisible to the quality tuple).

4. **31 of 73 NHL puck-line picks carried no line.** Stored as
   `'Buffalo Sabres PL'` with `line=None` while the same row held
   `close_puckline -1.5`. The label builder is not at fault — replayed
   against the stored row it returns `('Buffalo Sabres -1.5', 'HOME',
   -1.5)`. The pick was resolved before odds landed and `pick_lock`
   preserved the unlined copy on every later pass, so the gap never
   healed. 26 were repairable from ctx on their own row;
   `fix_nhl_rl_pick_lines.py` restored them, re-deriving for the side
   already stored so a pick cannot change teams. Verified the convention
   before touching anything: `close_puckline` is home-perspective
   (agrees with the ML favourite on 244 of 249), and all 26 restored
   lines put the favourite laying -1.5 — 0 inconsistent.

**And the price was wrong by 290 points.** The POTD defaulted a side
market to -110. Tonight's POTD is Buffalo -1.5, whose stored
`close_puckline_home_price` is **+180**. A puck line is plus money for
the favourite (tonight: +180, +103, +170, +150) because it has to win by
two — the -110 default was imported from football/basketball spreads.
The Sharp had the same hole from the other direction: its NHL SELECT
shared NBA's column list, so `close_puckline_*_price` never arrived and
`receipt_invariants` refused both puck-line receipts as "a graded play
with no price is priced at a guess". Third explicit-SELECT blank in that
one block, after `close_home_ml` and `close_spread`.

## Model calibration audit (building Andy's personal card)

Checked every sport's `projected_spread` against its own market before
trusting any of it. Three different sign conventions are in play, and only
NBA/NCAAF are in `consistency_watchdog._PROJ_HOME_POSITIVE` — NCAAB and NHL
are absent, i.e. "not proven, do not check".

| sport | convention (from its writer) | corr(model, market) | compression | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| NBA | negative = home favoured | **+0.820** n=128 | −0.69 | calibrated |
| NCAAF | positive = home favoured | +0.651 n=576 | +1.17 | calibrated |
| NCAAB | positive = home wins by X | — | **+11.37** | **mis-scaled** |
| NHL | undeclared | **−0.467** n=245 | +0.74 | **unusable** |

**NCAAB's projection is mis-scaled, not contrarian.** Mean |model margin|
6.24 vs market 17.60. On games with a market margin ≥6 the model is short
of the market on **70 of 73 (95.9%)**, and it is under the total on **88 of
96 (91.7%)**, bias −6.2. A model that takes the dog 96% of the time and the
under 92% of the time has no opinion — it has a scale bug. This is the
likeliest reason no NCAAB signal has ever validated, and it is why the
engine's NCAAB picks look like they contradict their own projection: the
projection cannot express a 17-point favourite.

**NHL's projected_spread is anti-correlated with the market** (−0.467), so
its direction cannot be used at all. Matches the watchdog's note that
"NHL's writer is unsettled".

### Two bugs the wiring exposed, fixed before they wrote anything wrong

`ABBREV_ALIAS` was a flat NHL-only dict applied globally. Harmless with one
league, silently wrong the moment NBA arrived: `'LA' -> 'LAK'` is a Kings
mapping but in the NBA 'LA' is the Lakers or the Clippers, and
`'WAS' -> 'WSH'` renames a Wizards row to a Capitals code. Either one
attaches real money-flow numbers to the wrong team, which this file already
calls worse than no data. Now scoped per sport.

NBA then matched 10 of 34. The six missing codes were recovered by diffing
the two sets rather than guessing: site-not-in-ctx and ctx-not-in-site are
both exactly {GSW/GS, NOP/NO, NYK/NY, SAS/SA, UTA/UTAH, WAS/WSH} — a clean
bijection, every mapping 1:1. LAL/LAC, the pair that *could* be ambiguous,
already agree and were left alone. 10 -> **14 of 14**.

NCAAB has no abbrev column and 365 teams whose 2-4 letter codes are not
safely guessable (`CSU` already named the wrong school once). The logo's
`title` attribute carries the full mascot-bearing name ("Saint Joseph's
Hawks"), which is exactly what `ncaab_team_resolver` handles. Joined on the
resolved name: 0 -> **9 of 10 parsed**. The one miss is instructive —
MICHIGAN@CONNECTICUT on the site vs "UConn Huskies @ Michigan Wolverines" in
`line_history`, i.e. the two sources disagree on which team is home. A
flipped game would have put money on the wrong side, so refusing to match it
was correct.
