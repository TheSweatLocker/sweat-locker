## The live incident: Padres / Brewers across four surfaces

Andy: *"I see Padres ML on sweat card as prime (sentence starts out with
Brewers ML at +113…) then in game detail it says Brewers ML low conviction."*

**What the surfaces said at the time**

| surface | said |
|---|---|
| Sweat Card | San Diego Padres ML · PRIME · conviction 95 · **Brewers narrative attached** |
| `jerry_reads` (Game Detail) | AWAY / Brewers · conviction 0 |
| `primary_play` (game card) | AWAY / Brewers · COVERAGE · "⚠ Engine passed … 18% win prob · −45.9pp edge" |
| Game Detail read cache | HOME / Padres · **COVERAGE** ("low conviction") |

**Timeline that produced it**

```
12:58:44  primary_play = HOME/Padres/95, downgraded to COVERAGE by an
          LR-vs-consensus dissent rule
13:00:50  recompute_primary_play --force -> AWAY/Brewers/0, COVERAGE via the
          MC hard block (defensive_gates, pick_prob < 0.40)
13:01:39  generate_sweat_card read jerry_reads (still HOME/95) and published
          "San Diego Padres ML PRIME" carrying a short_read arguing BREWERS
later     reconcile_jerry_to_primary forced jerry_reads to AWAY/0 to match
          primary_play; prose left alone (documented behaviour)
```

Two consecutive computations of the same game produced **opposite sides two
minutes apart**, and the card froze the one in between.

**Why ordering could not fix it.** `mlb_pipeline.yml` runs reconcile before
`recompute_primary_play --force`, so the recompute invalidates the
reconciliation — and `daily_card.yml`, which published this card, never runs
reconcile at all.

**Fix (`4656e20b`)** — the card now takes side and tier from `primary_play`
(the authority named in reconcile's own docstring) and prose from
`jerry_reads`, and refuses to publish over a pass (COVERAGE/SKIP/PASS or an
"Engine passed" sub). One fewer dependency rather than one more guard.

**Root cause of the Brewers prose (`2b58f37a`)** — `analyst_facts` handed the
LLM a bare `projected_spread` with no team attached. On this game it was
**+0.33, which means the Padres by 0.33** (measured r=+0.2998 vs actual home
margin, n=89). The read quoted "+0.33" and argued the Brewers: right
magnitude, inverted sign. Now stated as "San Diego Padres by 0.33 runs".

`close_spread` runs the **opposite** direction (r=−0.1829) from the three
projections, which is exactly why a bare signed number in a prompt was a trap.

**Tonight's four surfaces were corrected by hand with Andy's approval** and
verified by read-back; `best_plays` convictions confirmed unchanged.

## Grading integrity

`audit_prop_grades.py` (`e35a7bad`) checks every published grade against
`mlb_player_game_log` / `nfl_player_stats` — tables the settler never reads,
so the verdict is independent of the code under audit.

```
MLB   87 wrong of 5,058 judged (1.72%)   correcting them: -11.303u
NFL   15 wrong of 1,337 judged (1.12%)   correcting them:  -0.502u
```

Not a uniform error rate — bad grading **runs** on particular dates. NFL: 12
of 15 on 09-13, written by `resolve_nfl_props.py` taking `m.iloc[-1]` (the
player's latest row of the season) so a Sunday-morning run graded week 1
against preseason volume. Disabled 09-19; **the rows it wrote were never
corrected**. MLB: 18 on 09-04, 12 on 09-11, 10 on 09-26.

**Correcting all 102 makes the published record ~11.8u WORSE.** Andy's call,
untouched.

Measured before trusting the flip direction (n=4,104, box-score adjudicated):
FADE stored results match the **flipped** side 93.8%; LEAN/STRONG/PRIME/BACK
match the prop side 97–99%. So the stored result is the side we *backed*.

## Tiering: what is actually wrong

The ladder **works** — hit rate declines monotonically across 744 clean picks
(PRIME 59.4% → STRONG 54.4% → LEAN 50.4%). It is not inverted.

What fails is price:

| tier | hit | market implies | edge | ROI | median odds |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| PRIME | 59.2% | **61.3%** | **−2.1pp** | −4.8% | −145 |
| STRONG | 54.4% | 55.2% | −0.8pp | −1.5% | −119 |
| LEAN | 50.4% | 54.9% | −4.5pp | −8.2% | −118 |
| COVERAGE | 60.4% | **49.0%** | **+11.3pp** | **+24.0%** | **+110** |

Same signature on sides+totals (PRIME 57.8% vs 57.9% implied). **Not a prop
problem — a tiering problem.** 26.4% of PRIME props sit at −200 or worse,
where the −250+ bucket returns 69.9% hit for −12.9% ROI.

**But the premise is only half-supported**, because of what price capture
turned out to be doing — see below. Three hypotheses were tested and failed:
edge-ranking (`conviction − implied` as a filter returns −6.3% vs −0.7% for
its complement; conviction is not a probability so the proxy is noise),
price-alone (no monotonic pattern), tier-within-price (cells of 30–50, noise).

`refit_conviction` does not help: **3.8% board coverage** (386 of 10,202 since
09-22), 100% on er/ks families and **0% on every high-volume batter family**,
flat ladder, and it is another *probability* estimate so it would rank the
same overpriced picks first. It lands in a different tier band than base
conviction **59.3% of the time** (median gap 18.3, max 69.6) — which is the
whole reason a PRIME can display a conviction of 24.

## The finding that reframes it: we publish worse than the price we saw

Of 408 reads carrying both a taken price and a best-available price:

```
pick price == best available     28  ( 7%)
pick price WORSE than best      380  (93%)
median give-up when worse       1.11pp of implied probability
```

PRIME's deficit is −2.1pp. We give up a median 1.11pp at the point of
recording. **Plausibly about half the shortfall is self-inflicted**, which is
a different fix from "pick better props".

Verified `pick_odds` is the **publish** price, not the close: 318 of 326
game_read receipts match `price_american`, **zero** match
`close_price_american`. Also: `close_price_american` is empty on **every**
read, so CLV and line movement are currently unmeasurable, and only 418 of
1,641 reads carry any price at all.

**So price capture comes before any tiering change.** Tuning selection against
a number that is partly a bookkeeping artifact would bake the artifact in.

## Corrections I had to make to my own reporting

Logged because the pattern matters more than the individual errors: in each
case I reported before checking what my own query had selected.

1. **"The tier ladder is INVERTED."** It is not. My join dropped **610 of
   1,216 receipts (50%)** because it could only see receipts whose source prop
   row still existed. The bias concentrated in STRONG — **88% of STRONG
   receipts had their source deleted**, leaving n=33 that happened to run
   45.5%. That one cell produced the "inversion". My second hypothesis (wrong
   tier field) was also wrong: receipt.tier == source.tier on **606 of 606**.
2. **"Two picks carried a different prop's conviction."** My own matching bug
   — card receipts store no `prop_type`, so I fell back to line+direction, and
   Chris Sale has both `bb_under 1.5` and `er_under 1.5`. Sale's published
   65/STRONG matches its own row exactly.

   inside it; −300 is the floor.
4. **"Priced in."** I said the market "already prices" PRIME. What I measured
   was arithmetic on our own rows: 59.2% delivered against 61.3% implied *at
   the prices we recorded*. The causal story was unearned — and the price-
   capture finding above shows at least one other explanation is live.
5. **MLB postseason Jerry reads were 7-6-1 / −12.4%.** Wrong window — 09-30
   excluded Wild Card Day 1, which went 4-0. Correct figure is **10-6-1,
