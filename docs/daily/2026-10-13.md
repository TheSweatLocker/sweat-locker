## FIXED · one absent column was dropping all 17 trend fields (MLB + NBA)

`enrich_team_trends` PATCHes 17 season-trend fields onto `<sport>_game_context`.
`mlb_game_context` and `nba_game_context` have no `*_season_ats_pushes` or
`*_season_ou_pushes` (NFL and NCAAF do), and **PostgREST rejects a PATCH naming
any absent column as a whole** — 400 PGRST204, all 17 fields discarded. So MLB
wrote nothing on every run while the upstream source was correct and current
(`team_season_trends` holds all 30 MLB teams for 2026, refreshed daily).

That is the root cause of 12 enabled MLB signals evaluating NULL:
`home/away_team_ats_hot_season`, `_ats_cold_season`,
`home/away_team_over_trend_season`, `_under_trend_season`,
`both_teams_over_trend_season`, `both_teams_under_trend_season`.

**`--dry-run` never issues the PATCH**, so the failure was invisible in exactly
the mode used to verify the job: dry-run printed `H=64.3% A=61.3%`, the live run
printed `0/2 patched`.

Fixed with a PGRST204 strip-retry (mirroring `nfl_game_context.upsert_context`)
that also prints which columns it stripped. MLB now 2/2 patched,
`home_season_cover_pct` = 64.3 / 61.2 where it was NULL. Migration
`20261013a_mlb_nba_season_push_columns.sql` adds the four columns; the
strip-retry makes it non-blocking.

### 2 · Mimicry: LEVELS looked independent, DEVIATIONS are not

My first pass reported v4 vs panel at **r = −0.001** and called them two
independent takes. That measured the wrong thing. The engine's consensus is
about agreeing on a side **relative to the line**, so what matters is the
deviation:

| | correlation (n=55) |
|---|---|
| levels — corr(v4, panel margin) | **+0.076** |
| **deviations — corr(v4−line, panel−line)** | **+0.836** |

Cause: `panel` regressed on the line has **slope +0.434** — compressed to 43%
of market scale (~2.3x under-projection, matching
`project_sp_plus_compression_927`), and v4 is a constant. Both deviations are
therefore dominated by `−line`, which is why they move together.

**Correlate DEVIATIONS, never levels, when asking whether models are
independent.**

### Item 3 · ANSWERED by measurement — and my recommendation was WRONG

I had proposed "scale panel's deviation by ~2.3x". That is backwards. If
panel = 0.477 × line − 0.18, its deviation from the line is
(0.477 − 1) × line + c = **−0.523 × line + c** — scaling that up *amplifies*
the artifact rather than correcting it.

The correct operation is to remove the line-predictable component:

    de-biased dev = (model − line) − E[(model − line) | line]

| model | on-line slope | compression | raw dev slope | de-biased slope |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| panel | +0.477 | 2.10× | +0.229 | **+0.019** |
| v3 | +0.499 | 2.00× | +0.414 | **+0.414** |
| mc | +0.783 | **1.28×** | +0.101 | +0.081 |

**Panel's apparent edge is entirely compression artifact** — de-biasing
collapses it to +0.019. **v3's is completely unaffected**, so its deviation is
genuine idiosyncratic disagreement, not a scale error. mc mostly survives and
is the best-calibrated model we have (1.28× vs panel's 2.10×).

So the action is **not** to rescale panel but to **exclude it from edge
logic**, which the corrected consensus already does. Item 3 therefore needs no
new code — it is independent mechanistic support for item 1, and it explains
*why* the v4/panel deviation correlation was +0.925: both deviations were
dominated by the same `−line` compression term.

**Generalisable rule: before treating a model's disagreement with the market
as an edge, regress the model on the line and remove the predictable part. If
the edge does not survive, it was compression.**

| 10 | 49 | **32.7%** | **−37.7%** | [−61.0, −14.3] * |

It gets *worse* as the gap grows — the opposite of what the slope implies.

Splitting by whether SOR agrees with the market settles the mechanism:

| cell | n | cover% | ROI | 95% CI |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| SOR **agrees** with the market | 3363 | 50.0% | **−4.6%** | [−7.6, −1.3] * |
| SOR **disagrees** | 880 | 52.2% | −0.4% | [−6.7, +5.9] |
| agrees, \|gap\| ≥ 5 | 1536 | 50.1% | −4.3% | [−9.0, +0.4] |
| **disagrees, \|gap\| ≥ 5** | 85 | **40.0%** | **−23.6%** | [−43.9, −3.4] * |

When SOR agrees with the line you are paying vig to bet the favourite
(−4.6%, CI clears). When it disagrees sharply the market is right and SOR is
wrong (−23.6%, CI clears). There is no form in which backing it wins.

**WHY THE SLOPE MISLED ME, quantified.** +0.102 residual points per point of
SOR gap, against a median |gap| of 3.83, is about **0.4 points of expected
edge**. At −110 on a sub-7 line that is nowhere near enough to clear the
juice. The association is real and the rule is still unprofitable, because a
slope measures a continuous relationship while a bet needs 52.38% from a sign
decision.

**The lesson, which generalises past SOR:** a residual slope whose CI excludes
zero is necessary but nowhere near sufficient. Convert it to the actual rule —
side, threshold, price — before calling it an edge. On this page that step
turned a headline finding into a refutation, and it is the step that would
have shipped a losing signal.

I am NOT pivoting to the inverse (fading big SOR-vs-line disagreements shows
+23.6% by symmetry on n=85). That is a post-hoc sign flip on one cell found by
scanning seven thresholds, which is exactly the multiplicity trap this file
spent the day avoiding elsewhere.

### Measured: they are worse than the market they reproduce

The lasso falsified that claim by zeroing the non-market half.

## SCOPE · point-in-time team-feature history

Three separate attempts today died on the same constraint, which makes this the
highest-leverage remaining project:

| attempt | died on |
|---|---|
| SOR as a signal | stored columns start 2026-10-10; 14 NFL / 45 NCAAF graded rows |
| football LR retrain | NFL has 79 past context rows against a 300 minimum |
| NCAAF cover model | 203 training rows vs 17 features → +12.1pp overfit |

**The problem.** `*_game_context` holds only the current season and is written
forward — 316 NFL rows (123 past), 536 NCAAF (472 past). Results tables hold
years (NFL 7,550, NCAAF 15,647) but carry market data and scores only. So every
team feature we own exists for ~6 weeks of history, and nothing that needs team
features can be validated.

**What makes this tractable:** `compute_margin_strength.py` already proves the
pattern. It rebuilds SOR/SOS walk-forward from results alone and has a
`--validate` mode that rates only on games strictly prior. That is exactly the
shape a feature-history builder needs.

**Proposed scope, in order:**

1. **Decide the feature set from what is reconstructible.** Anything derivable
   from `*_game_results` (margins, scores, dates, opponents) can be rebuilt
   as-of any date: SRS/SOR, SOS, rolling form, ATS/OU records, rest, H2H. Things
   sourced from a third-party snapshot (SP+, talent, returning production) cannot
   be rebuilt and must be excluded or back-sourced separately.
2. **Build `build_feature_history.py`** — for each (sport, game), compute the
   feature vector using ONLY games before it, and write to a new
   `*_feature_history` table keyed by game_id. One row per game, immutable once
   written.
3. **Validate the reconstruction against the live columns** on the ~6 weeks where
   both exist. If reconstructed SOR on 2026-10-04 does not match what was
   stored that day, the rebuild is wrong and nothing downstream is trustworthy.
   This is the gate.
4. **Then re-run the three dead attempts** against the history: the SOR rule, a
   football LR on real features, and the NCAAF cover model. Each already has a
   written, validated test harness — they only lacked rows.

**Honest expectation.** This unlocks *validation*, not edge. Everything measured
today says the market is efficient against what we own; a longer history makes
the answer trustworthy rather than making it positive. The value is that we stop
shipping on 79-row samples and stop retiring ideas that were only
under-powered.

**Cost:** the reconstruction is one script plus a validation gate. Step 3 is the
one that can fail, and it should be run before any modelling work is scheduled
on top.

ATS + O/U records, `resid_l5`, rest_days, h2h.

**Excluded rather than imputed** — third-party snapshots with no as-of-date

### Step 2 — `build_feature_history.py` + `20261013b_feature_history.sql`

Reuses `compute_margin_strength`'s `fit_srs` / `sos_from` / `load_games`
instead of reimplementing. That matters most for the sign rule —
`close_spread` is the HOME handicap in every sport **except NFL** where it is
the AWAY line (n=7,325). `load_games` returns `home_line` already corrected
and this module uses only that.

**The invariant and the proof.** Every feature uses only games that finished
strictly earlier, same season. Games are grouped by DATE and rated on earlier
dates only, so a Sunday slate sees Thursday and before and same-day games
cannot see each other; seasons never mix.

`--leak-test` builds twice (truncated at a cut date, and complete) and
compares every row dated ≤ the cut:

| sport | cut | rows compared | result |
|---|---|---|---|
| NFL | 2025-11-01 | 122 | identical |
| NCAAF | 2025-10-15 | 13,189 | identical |

And the test is **not vacuous**: against a deliberately leaky full-season fit,
**0 of 221** fed 2025 NFL rows match (mean \|gap\| 1.556 pts, max 7.72). Week-1
rows carry `home_srs` NULL.

`*_fed` flags rather than imputed zeros, per the 10/12 prop-projection lesson:
a row with two games of history is not a row with a small rating, it is a row
with **no** rating.
