## The method lesson, which matters more than the finding

**Walk-forward validation cannot detect a leaked FEATURE.** It guards
threshold choice against the future. If the input contains the outcome, every
period is contaminated equally and the harness prints `STABLE EDGE` — which
it did, on every contaminated cut, with a monotonic ladder and a symmetric
control. Stability is the leak's *signature* here, not evidence against it.

The two checks that do work:
1. **Era-split on the fix date.** A fix bounds new writes; it almost never
   repairs stored ones.
2. **Rebuild the feature from raw components and re-run.** The gap between
   stored-feature and rebuilt-feature results *is* the leak, measured.

Cheapest tell, checkable in one query: **does the edge grow as the price gets
cheaper?** Leaky ran +5.3 at ≤−250 up to +22.0 at ≥−110. Rebuilt is flat
(−2.5 / −4.4 / −6.5 / −1.9 / −0.4). Real skill does not scale inversely with
market confidence.

Saved as `feedback_walk_forward_misses_feature_leak`.

## VALIDATED: wind ≥ 12 → UNDER

Sign re-established on this table first rather than imported:
`HOME_HANDICAP_SIGN = -1.0` reproduces `spread_result` on **7,181 of 7,181**,
and home favourites win 67.4% outright vs home dogs 35.3%. `game_type` is REG
+ playoffs, so no preseason contamination.

```
threshold walk (outdoor, wind recorded):
  wind >= 6    52.4%  n=3592   ROI  -0.03%
  wind >= 10   54.9%  n=1882   ROI  +4.88%   CI clears
  wind >= 12   56.1%  n=1286   ROI  +7.18%   CI clears
  wind >= 15   57.0%  n=674    ROI  +8.76%   CI clears
  wind >= 18   59.5%  n=289    ROI +13.62%   CI clears

CONTROL:  wind < 12   49.3%  n=3879   ROI -5.85%
          wind < 10   48.8%  n=3283   ROI -6.90%

WALK-FORWARD BY SEASON (26 periods), threshold FIXED:
  655-519   55.8%   CI[52.9, 58.6]   17 of 26 seasons over BE
  VERDICT: STABLE EDGE
```

Four reasons this is not the usual false positive:

1. **The control loses.** Low wind is 49.3% — the cut has a direction and its
   opposite is negative, so it isn't a population artefact.
2. **Fill bias ruled out.** `wind` is NULL on domes by design (1,196 dome +
   625 closed roof) and recorded on **95% of outdoor games**. The comparison
   is outdoor-high vs outdoor-low, not outdoor vs dome.
3. **The threshold was walked, not picked** — monotone 52.4% → 59.5%, no
   collapse past the cut.
4. **Independent replication.** `project_wind_under_lens_1009` measured 55.6%
   n=1,444 from a different source. Two samples at 55.6% and 56.1% beats one
   cell clearing a correction.

## Third independent confirmation: don't let it re-tune itself

```
threshold FIXED    655-519  55.8%  17 of 26 seasons
threshold LEARNED  412-357  53.6%  14 of 26 seasons
```

A 2.2-point penalty for re-picking the cutoff each season — after the fade's
8-point penalty (fixed 54-58% vs learned 47.7%). **Any "engine recalibration"
that re-fits thresholds per period should be expected to lose ground.** Fix
thresholds from the full sample; let the engine switch between fixed,
pre-validated modes.

## Repair applied

Same method as the team side: find the dataset nobody queried, then test it

## The dataset

**`nfl_player_stats` holds 160,417 player-weeks, 1999–2026, 6,248 players**,
core volume stats at 100% fill, plus `target_share`, `wopr`,
`air_yards_share`, `racr`, `pacr`, `passing_epa`.

Two data facts found before using it:

- **2025+ loads defensive players** (DB 4,249, LB 3,451, DL 3,202) while

- **`player_name` is not a key.** "J.Williams" week 1 is three distinct
  `player_id`s on three different teams; "Jordan Phillips" is a BUF player
  and a MIA player. 151 names map to multiple ids, 1,770 names are stored
  abbreviated. It bites only **19 of 4,301 graded props (0.4%)**, so it is a
  latent hazard rather than a live defect — but joins must use `player_id`.

Every window is bounded strictly before the prop's (season, week), ordered
from `nfl_game_results`' own (season, week) → date map rather than guessed.

## A false positive I caught, and the correction that rescued a real one

Pooled raw, two features looked like the lever:

```
rate (per-opportunity)  +0.944  CI[+0.406,+1.483]   "ADDS INFORMATION"
opps (recent volume)    +0.108  CI[+0.003,+0.214]   "ADDS INFORMATION"
```

Neither cleared inside **any** single family — the tell. `rate` mixes
yards-per-carry (~4), catch rate (~0.7) and yards-per-attempt (~7) against
residuals spanning ±50 for passing yards and ±2 for receptions, so pooling
draws a line through the family means. Z-scoring both sides within family:

```
rate              +0.016  CI[-0.030,+0.061]   nothing
opps              -0.000  CI[-0.045,+0.045]   nothing
opponent-allowed  +0.022  CI[-0.022,+0.065]   nothing
target_share      -0.015  CI[-0.058,+0.029]   nothing
wopr              -0.021  CI[-0.064,+0.022]   nothing
l5 - line         +0.061  CI[+0.018,+0.104]   ADDS INFORMATION
```

The same correction cut both ways: it killed two artefacts and **rescued a
real effect**. `l5 − line` was +0.058 with a CI spanning 0 when pooled raw;
every family's slope was positive (receptions +0.144, rushing +0.103,
completions +0.093, passing +0.064, attempts +0.097, pass_tds +0.144) with
each CI individually spanning zero, and correct aggregation clears it.

Saved as `feedback_pooled_slope_across_scales_is_fake`.

## What it means in betting terms

A standardised slope of 0.061 is a correlation of ~0.06. The betting test
agrees it is not enough: best cut (l10 vs line, gap ≥10%) is **−0.1 vs
breakeven**, everything else −0.3 to −3.1. Real, and too small for the vig.

## So: the answer to the question

Two weeks is ~14 NFL games. A rule chosen on 14 and confirmed on 14 carries
a coin-flip's worth of evidence, and both sports demonstrate it concretely —
one rule passed and loses long-run, another passed spectacularly and loses,
a third inverted on contact. **Tune-and-confirm on adjacent weeks cannot
distinguish an edge from a streak.** It is the same failure as the adaptive
fade (55.7% fitted → 47.7%) and the re-learned wind threshold (55.8% →
53.6%), and it is why the 28-season and 160k-player-week datasets matter

## Three things that matter more than the headline

**1. The winning simple rule is OPPOSITE by sport.** NFL away 72.9%, NCAAF
home 58.4%. A global "take the away side" would be actively wrong on the
sport carrying 6x the volume — same shape as money-flow inverting by sport.

**2. The magnitude is 2026-specific; the indictment is not.** NFL away over
28 seasons is 51.1%, so 72.9% is a hot run rather than a law. That cuts
against the engine, not for it: **45.8% is below what random side-picking
gives.** Whether away is 51% or 73% long-run, we are underperforming a coin
flip on games we selected ourselves.

**3. SIDE selection is the defect, not GAME selection.** We picked away on
only 154 of 405 (38%). On NCAAF, home covered **58.4% on the games we
chose** — the selection is finding live games and the side is going the wrong
way. That is independent confirmation of the flip finding (flipped curated
football spreads = 65.3%) from a second direction, and consistent with tier
being applied backwards.

Disagreement split on the 405 curated picks: both right 16.5%, **only engine
right 32.3%**, only away rule right 29.6%, both wrong 21.5%. The engine is
*not* uninformative — it is nearly as often uniquely right as the rule is.
It loses the aggregate because the side skews wrong. That is a much more
fixable problem than "the model has no signal".
