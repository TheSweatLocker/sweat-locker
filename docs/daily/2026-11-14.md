## 04:11 ET — the day-over-day read did not exist, and could not have worked

### What was already there, and what each one missed

| tool | covers | misses |
|---|---|---|
| `morning_audit.py` | 30-day board, per surface per sport | a 30-day window cannot show last night — one bad night moves a 129-pick denominator by under a point |
| `compute_surface_records.py` | all 25 surfaces x 8 sports, the single source of truth for what counts as a pick | windows are mtd/prev_month/d7/d30/epoch/lifetime. **No daily one** |
| `aggregate_daily_records.py` | per-(surface, sport, date) rows | 6-10 rows a night across 12 surfaces, and the set is missing **sweat_card** (the dashboard), **game_read** (our highest-volume surface, 538-386 over 30d) and **every `<sport>_sides` surface** |

### The reason no record surface could ever have shown it

Every picker in `compute_surface_records` filters `result=not.is.null`. That
is correct for a record — you cannot sum an ungraded pick — but it means an
**ungraded pick is invisible to every record surface we own.** A night that
graded 14 of its 60 picks reports the 14 and says nothing about the 46, so a
BROKEN night renders as a THIN night.

Measured on 2026-11-13 at 04:11 ET: **72 of 1,583 receipts ungraded**,
including `game_read` NCAAB 46 of 60 and **100% of the NBA slate** (9
game_read, 6 fade, 1 sharp_card).

## Root causes, in the order they bite

### 1. Receipt grading ran BEFORE the finals it needed existed

`grade_public_receipts` is sequenced in `nightly_cross_sport.sh`, which
`mlb_grade_overnight` fires at 01:10-04:10 UTC. That script resolves
NCAAF/NFL/NHL finals **itself** and then grades — but NBA and NCAAB finals
are resolved in **their own pipelines, hours later** (11:23 and 12:13 UTC).

So for the two sports actually in season, receipt grading always ran before
the scores landed, and their receipts waited for the **next** night's pass —
a full 24h late. NBA lag between `game_date` and `resolved_at` ran **1-3
days on all 18 dates this season, never 0.**

This is the same fault `grade_jerry_reads` was moved for on 2026-09-08
("so NBA doesn't wait on the MLB nightly step"). The **receipts** grader
never got the same treatment. `[[project_mlb_pipeline_yml_owns_shared_surfaces_1103]]`

**Fixed:** `grade_public_receipts` + `grade_ledger_snapshots` now run in
`nba_pipeline.yml` and `ncaab_pipeline.yml` immediately behind each sport's
own resolver. Sport-agnostic and idempotent, so running in both places is
safe.

### 2. ESPN uses `state='post'` for a POSTPONED game

`resolve_ncaab_results` tested `state == 'post'` as "played". ESPN returns,
verbatim, for three games on 2026-11-14:

```
state=post   name=STATUS_POSTPONED   completed=False   scores=['0','0']
```

So a postponed game arrived as a **final with a 0-0 score**, and
`compute_outcome_patch` does not sanity-check a score — it derived
`home_win=False`, `spread_result='away_covered'`, `total_result='under'` for
games nobody played. **Any pick on one grades as a loss on a fabricated
result.**

I wrote those 3 rows myself with a wide `--days-back` pass this morning, and
caught them in the next dry run. No other sport has a single 0-0 row
(checked NCAAF/NFL/NBA/NHL/MLB, 2,568 rows) — NCAAB is where early-season
postponements live.

**Fixed in all four writers**, because one writer patched is not a fix
(`[[feedback_fix_every_writer_to_the_table]]`): `resolve_ncaab_results`,
`ncaab_backfill_results`, `ncaaf_results_espn`, `ufc_grader`. All now
require `type.completed`, plus a 0-0 plausibility clause — no basketball
game ends 0-0, so a 0-0 "final" is a status we misread, not a result
(`[[feedback_model_output_needs_a_plausibility_guard]]`).

### 3. One neutral-site game, two rows, line and score split

13 NCAAB games from 11-01..11-12 survived a full `--days-back 14` pass still
unscored — Kentucky/Kansas, UCLA/Arizona, Villanova/Notre Dame, Houston/Cal.
Not a reach problem, and not a name problem.

`game_id` embeds the orientation (`ncaab_<date>_<away>_<home>`). **At a
neutral site there is no home team**, so ESPN and the odds feed pick
opposite ones, neither id matched, and the game was INSERTED a second time:

```
ours (odds feed)    close_spread 7.5    home_score NULL
ESPN's (inserted)   close_spread NULL   74-72
```

The line sits on a row that never gets a score; the score sits on a row with
no line. **Neither grades.** Season to date: **14 fixtures, 28 rows.** All
13 of the split ones carry `is_neutral_site = True` in
`ncaab_game_context` — 13 of 13. The dates disagree too (ctx 11-02 vs
results 11-03 on UCLA/Arizona).

**Fixed both ways:**

- `resolve_ncaab_results` gains a third resolution step after the id and
  legacy-id lookups: match the unordered team pair within ±1 day and remap
  onto the stored row. Scores are reassigned **by team name, never by
  position** — copying home onto home would hand the home side the away
  team's points and grade the opposite side of what we published
  (`[[feedback_grade_the_side_we_published]]`). The name test must mirror
  exactly in both directions, and >1 candidate refuses rather than guesses.
  After: `existing rows matched 681/681` (was 659) and **inserted 0** (was 22).
- `repair_ncaab_fixture_integrity.py` (new) clears both defects on stored
  data. Merge direction is not arbitrary: **the score moves onto the LINED
  row**, because ESPN will hand back a final any time and a closing spread
  is gone once the book takes it down. The duplicate is then deleted even
  when it carries its own line — after a merge both rows would be scored AND
  lined, and every record derived from this table would count the game
  twice. The discarded line is printed (2 of 4 were exact mirrors of the
  kept one: Nebraska -2.5 = Providence +2.5).

Verified the arithmetic independently before writing. Houston @ California,
home line **+19.5**, Cal lost by 18 → `home_covered`. Kansas **-5.5** won by
20 → `home_covered`. Both correct.

**NCAAB unscored: 59 → 0. NBA: 9 → 0.** Repair is idempotent.

### 4. `--days-back 2` could not reach a night it missed twice

The scheduled NCAAB resolver ran `--days-back 2`, so a game that failed to
resolve twice was unreachable forever by the cron. That is not what caused
the 13 (see above), but recovering 11-13's 46 finals needed a hand run with
a wider window. Now **`--days-back 4`**, for three extra ESPN calls.

### 5. A graded parlay whose receipt stayed NULL

12 `ledger` receipts carried NULL while **all 12 of their snapshots were
graded W/L**. `backfill_public_receipts` carries `result` in its payload and
then dedupes on what is already stored — run today: *"345 reconstructed, 345
already live, 0 to write."* So a receipt written before its snapshot graded
keeps a NULL result forever, and a parlay cannot be graded the way
`grade_public_receipts` grades everything else.

**Fixed:** `inherit_from_source()` in `grade_public_receipts.py`, which is
what that file's own opening line always claimed happened ("most surfaces
inherit a grade from the table they were reconstructed from"). Confirmed by
readback, not by a 2xx. **12 inherited, ledger ungraded 12 → 0.**

The ledger backlog also needed its own join fixed: `_fetch_result` gains the
same orientation-free fallback. The 11-12 note was right that a grader must
not flip home/away — but nothing needs to: `_resolve_pick_side` already
matches the leg's **team name** against the row's own orientation, so the
row only needed FINDING, not flipping. `Saint Joseph's @ South Florida` is
stored one way in results and the other in context, and it was blocking four
chalk_parlay snapshots. **graded 4, skipped 0.** Sides verified: id=476
"South Florida ML" → LOSS, id=477 "Saint Joseph's ML" → WIN, against 80-83.

## The deliverable: `daily_performance_report.py`

Reports over the **same 25 pickers** `compute_surface_records` defines, on a
one-day window — imported, not restated, because a second definition of a
surface is how two codepaths over one feed start to disagree.

1. **Is the day complete?** Finals per sport first (it is usually the cause),
   then ungraded picks per (surface, sport). A pick is only **OVERDUE** once
   its sport's finals are all in — props written days before kickoff are
   legitimately ungraded, and NFL props run 455 of 495 ungraded on a
   Thursday on games nobody has played.
2. **The day**, per sport per surface.
3. **Where the money is going**, d30, ranked.

**No rate under n=30.** The house rule binds hardest here: a single day
almost never reaches n=30 in one cell, so the day column prints W-L-P and
units and **no percentage**. Rates appear only in d7/d30 and only where that
window carries n>=30.

Pickers run concurrently (`prop_coverage` alone walks ~29k rows): **2m45s →
1m41s**. Serial it hit the morning audit's flat 120s timeout and printed
"(timed out)" — the one output a completeness gate must never produce, being
indistinguishable from a clean night. `_run_script` now takes a per-script
timeout.

**Exit 0 = the day is complete, 1 = provisional, 2 = ungraded with finals in.**
As of this morning 2026-11-13 reports **COMPLETE, 1,583 picks, 0 ungraded,
exit 0** — the first time a night has been fully settled at cadence time.

## The morning audit stopped crying wolf

Run at 04:11 ET it reported **4 CRITICALs and exit 2** for a day whose
builds were still six hours away. A check that is red every night is a check
nobody reads (`[[feedback_a_detector_must_know_the_intentional_case]]`).

- **Build times are now known.** Measured from `jerry_cache.created_at` over
  11-06..11-13 rather than guessed: `best_bet` lands 12:41-13:44 UTC,
  `sharp_card` 12:57-14:46 UTC. Before that, missing reads **"not built yet
  — due in 5h57m"**, not CRITICAL. Only ever for TODAY; a past date missing a
  surface is missing it for good. Grace is 120m because
  `nba_pipeline.yml`'s own comment measures a 09:00 ET cron landing at
  "~1:24pm ET at median lateness".
- **Out-of-season is not an outage.** `mlb_slate_loaded` was CRITICAL ("Games
  tab will be empty") for a sport whose season ended **2026-11-05**. Now
  gated on `sport_registry.season_start/season_end`, returning None if the
  registry is unreadable so it can never silence a real outage.
- **Both `_et_today()` helpers had the hardcoded `-4`.** In `morning_audit`
  and `pipeline_smoke_test` — the two tools read to decide whether the
  overnight worked. A fixed -4 reads a DAY AHEAD during 04:00-04:59 UTC on
  the 127 EST days a year, and `mlb_grade_overnight`'s crons land in that
  hour. Both now delegate to `sweat_dates`
  (`[[feedback_hardcoded_et_offset_breaks_in_est]]`).

Smoke test: **3 critical + 3 warnings → 0 critical, 1 warning.**

The daily board is wired into `morning_audit` ahead of the 30-day records,
since it decides whether those records are complete. It also runs
automatically in `nba_pipeline.yml` — NBA is the last sport to settle each
night, so ~07:15 ET is the first moment a complete cross-sport day exists.

with. The marker is deliberately coarse — `v_*_props_publishable` is where
