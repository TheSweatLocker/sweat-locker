# 2026-11-22 · Sunday

## 1. The loudest test in the check had no signal, and it buried the one that did

`card_self_contradiction` had been CRITICAL continuously since **2026-10-25**
(backlog R66) and the daily log recorded it "untouched" on 11-04, 11-09, 11-14
and 11-21. Four weeks of red. The reason was not a hard problem — it was that
nobody had ever asked whether its tests were any good.

So every test was replayed over all stored context (2026-08-21 .. 2027-01-10,
88 dates) and graded from final scores. Moneyline buckets are priced at the
close, because a win rate is a favourite/dog mix and not a record
(`feedback_an_unpriced_moneyline_rate_is_not_a_record`).

| test | fires | of | flagged outcome | clean outcome | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| COVER | 188 | 375 (50.1%) | 48.6% n=105 | 51.2% n=170 | no signal |
| SIDE | 30 | 165 (18.2%) | −5.70u n=28 | −1.03u n=124 | mix artifact |
| PRICE | 49 | 216 (22.7%) | 62.5%, −0.9% ROI n=40 | 53.7%, −8.2% n=136 | **backwards** |
| FLIP | 6 | — | 6 of 6 verified wrong-sided | — | **real** |
| STALE | 30 | — | 29 genuine, 1 false | — | real, drift's job |

**COVER fired on half the published spread card** and the picks it flagged won
at the same rate as the picks it passed. No threshold rescues it: the ≥3.0pt
bucket is 44.3% on n=61, inside one standard error of 50%.

**SIDE looked like the real thing** — 61.3% (n=124) where it agrees against
41.4% (n=29) where it fires. That gap is the **favourite/dog mix**: 111/13
against 13/15. Priced at the close it is −1.03u against −5.70u on n=28, inside
one standard deviation, and under the house n≥30 gate.

**PRICE runs backwards.** The ML picks it calls "laying a price our own model
says is too short" returned **−0.9% ROI on n=40** against −8.2% on n=136 for
the ones it passes. Not significant either way, but there is no version of
this where it earns a publish-block.

The common thread: all three ask a **second model to ratify a pick the first
one made**. `projected_spread` and the LR probability are not what chose most
of these picks, so disagreeing with them is a cross-model difference of
opinion, not a pick contradicting itself
(`feedback_lens_independence_needs_different_inputs`,
`feedback_a_derived_column_can_hold_two_models`).

### What was underneath

Six published picks that named a side the closing moneyline priced the other
way — including **`BAL +8.5` on a team the book had at −375**, at STRONG tier.
FLIP had been reporting them all along, 6 lines inside a payload capped at 25
that COVER filled first (`feedback_never_measure_from_truncated_output`).

All six are NFL and every one is accounted for:

The three preseason rows predate `ensemble_scorer`'s 2026-09-11 NFL
`close_spread` sign fix. **Zero unexplained rows after that date** — there is
no live unmarked writer doing this.

The `_oc_flipped` row is the one I fixed at source yesterday, and it shows
**what yesterday's backfill could not reach**: the repair only re-signs rows
where `|label| == |line|` and the signs differ. Here the sport-blind
`float(close_spread)` made the label and the line agree — *both wrong*. It
classifies as 'ok' and is left alone. Left alone on purpose, now that it is
understood: the label is what Andy published and what the grader settled, so
rewriting it would be rewriting history.

### Four fixes

1. **FLIP is now the only blocking kind.** COVER, SIDE, PRICE, STALE and
   BOARD are reported at WARNING with the measurement quoted in the check.
   FLIP is not a prediction claim at all — it says the number we published
   names a different bet than the board, which is true or false whatever the
   result. That is what a publish-blocker should be.
2. **FLIP is arbitrated by the closing moneyline**, which reads no spread
   column and no per-sport sign table, so it cannot inherit the convention
   bug it is there to catch — this check has had **three** of those in its own
   history. A sign difference the ML *agrees* with is now `BOARD`: the stored
   `close_spread` contradicting its own price, which is a column defect, not
   a pick defect. 2 rows, both near-pick'em NCAAF.
   - Requiring **both** signals also means a corrupt price cannot block on its
     own. PHI @ JAX (10-11) carries `open −148/+124` then `close +960/−2400`
     on a game whose spread moved −1.5 → +1.5; the ML columns on that row are
     junk. It lands as STALE, not FLIP, because the spread agrees with our
     side. **Separate finding, worth a look.**
3. **Check B no longer runs on moneyline picks.** `Marshall ML` (10-31,
   STRONG) carried a residual `line` of 2.5 from the reroute that chose the
   ML, and the test read it as the bet — reported FLIP when the board and the
   pick agreed (Marshall was the −146 favourite and the spread had them −2.5).
4. **STALE no longer cites a number the refresher proved unavailable.**
   `South Carolina +15` fired last night as "the board now offers +16.5 — we
   are holding a WORSE number", hours after `refresh_unposted_pick_lines`
   moved that pick 16.5 → 15.0 having checked **seven books** and recorded
   "no book was posting the published number". `close_spread` still held 16.5.
   The check was reporting the refresher's own fix as the defect and offering
   a number proven to be on no board
   (`feedback_a_dead_value_keyed_on_itself_is_immortal`).

Also fixed: a COVER favourite whose projection is negative was printed as
"ahead by only −7.3", a signed number dropped into an "ahead by" clause.

### The policy is now a test

`test_card_self_contradiction.py`, 12/12, wired into `daily_card.yml`
immediately before the watchdog it guards. It pins the two things a later edit
would undo by accident: FLIP is the only blocking kind, and the favourite is
named by the moneyline, never by a spread column.

Scoped deliberately narrow — one module, no DB — and run under `--detector`,
so a failure is recorded as a finding and the card still ships. A broad
"run every test" step here would be the compileall mistake from 11-20 all over
again (`feedback_a_guards_blast_radius`).
